Stellar Nucleosynthesis

Investigation: Construct an Explanation

The Utah SEEd standard CHEM.1.4 asks you to construct an explanation about how fusion forms new elements with greater or lesser nuclear stability, with an emphasis on nuclear binding energy. Use the three panels above to investigate each of the activities below, then write your explanation in the final box.

  1. Activity 1 — Find the pattern

    In the Fusion Bench, try these five reactions. For each, record whether energy was released or required, and the value in MeV per nucleon:

    • H-2 + H-3
    • He-4 + C-12
    • C-12 + C-12
    • Si-28 + Si-28
    • Fe-56 + He-4

    What pattern do you notice? Look at where each reaction sits on the Binding Energy Curve. Which side of Fe-56 releases energy, and which side requires it?

  2. Activity 2 — The beryllium puzzle

    Try fusing He-4 + He-4 in the Fusion Bench. The result is surprising — Be-8 is actually slightly less stable per nucleon than He-4, so this reaction requires a tiny bit of energy. And yet, stars are full of carbon. How?

    Try the next step: fuse Be-8 + He-4. What happens? This three-step pathway is called the triple-alpha process. Why do stars need it to make carbon?

  3. Activity 3 — Why iron stops everything

    In the Stellar Nucleosynthesis tracker, walk through the burning stages until you reach the iron core at the center. Then go back to the Fusion Bench and try Fe-56 + He-4. What does the result tell you about why massive stars can't keep fusing past iron — and why the iron core eventually collapses?

  4. Activity 4 — Where did your gold come from?

    In the Stellar Nucleosynthesis tracker, click ⚡ Trigger Supernova after reaching the iron core. Notice the heavy elements that get forged in the explosion. The gold in jewelry, the iodine in your thyroid, and the uranium in nuclear reactors all have something in common: none of them were made inside a normal star. Where did they actually come from?

  5. ✍️ Activity 5 — Construct your explanation

    Combine what you've observed into a paragraph that explains all four of the following:

    1. How does fusion form new elements?
    2. Why does fusion release energy in some cases and require energy in others?
    3. What role does nuclear binding energy play in determining which is which?
    4. Why do supernovae need to occur to create elements heavier than iron?

    Your teacher will have a worksheet or document where you write your answer. Use the simulation panels above as evidence.
